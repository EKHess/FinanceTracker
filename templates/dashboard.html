How the TFSA calculator works

How your contributions grow and how the taxable comparison is estimated.

Growing your investment

The calculator starts with the amount already invested, then adds your ongoing contribution at the end of every selected contribution period. Annual return is divided by the number of periods per year—1 annually, 4 quarterly, 12 monthly, 26 biweekly, or 52 weekly—and the balance compounds once per period.

Periodic rate = annual return ÷ periods per yearFuture value = P × (1 + r)N + C × (((1 + r)N − 1) ÷ r)

In the formula, P is the starting contribution, C is each recurring contribution, r is the periodic return, and N is the total number of periods. At a 0% return, the result is simply the starting amount plus every contribution.

TFSA (tax-free)

The starting contribution, every recurring contribution, and all investment growth remain in the account. No income tax is deducted from the interest earned, so the full compounded balance is shown by the green bars.

Taxable investment account

The same contribution and compounding calculation is used first. The calculator then treats only the investment interest—not your contributed principal—as additional taxable income.

It uses the selected country, province or region, and tax year to calculate tax on your annual gross income both with and without that interest. The difference is deducted from the investment balance. Because the existing progressive tax rules are reused, interest that crosses a tax-bracket boundary is split across the applicable brackets.

These results are estimates for planning purposes. They use the tax rulesets configured in Income Sources and assume recurring contributions are made at the end of each period.
